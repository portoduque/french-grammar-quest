Whatever, whenever, wherever, whoever
The present subjunctive is used with the indefinite expressions that are the French
equivalents of whatever, whenever, wherever, and whoever.
  When the English whatever is followed by subject + verb, use the neuter quoi
que + present subjunctive.

      Quoi que tu fasses, je t’aimerai       Whatever you do, I will always love
           toujours.                              you.
      Quoi qu’elle dise, je ne changerai pas  Whatever she says, I will not change m
           d’avis.                              mind.

  When the English whatever is followed by a noun, use quel que (quelle que,
quels que, quelles que) + the subjunctive of être + noun. Quel que agrees in gender
and number with the noun that follows it.

      Quel que soit votre conseil, je prendrai  Whatever your advice, I will make a
         rendez-vous avec le patron.             appointment with the boss.      Quelle que soit leur décision, elle       Whatever their decision, she will
          démissionnera.                             resign.

  Look at the following examples that express wherever (où que) in French:

    Où que tu sois, appelle-moi.      Wherever you are, call me.
    Où qu’elle aille, il ira aussi.       Wherever she goes, he will go, too.

  The subjunctive is also used following the expression of whoever (qui que). For

Subordinate clauses
While studying all the units in the book, you ran into many different clauses. Let’s
recapitulate the categories so you can easily grasp their nature and their function.
Generally speaking, the purpose of any subordinate clause is to explain or present a
situation or a set of circumstances implied by the main clause.The causal clause
The causal clause expresses the natural link between cause and effect. This clause is
preceded by a conjunction such as the following: car, parce que, plus... plus,
moins... moins, puisque, étant donné que, vu que, du fait que, du moment que,
dès l’instant que, dès lors que, d’autant (plus) que, surtout que, soit que... soit
que, non que.

        J’ai réservé mes prochaines vacances sur   I made reservations for my next
           Internet, parce que c’est beaucoup plus    vacation online, because it is
           rapide.                           much faster.
      Dès lors qu’elle peut jardiner, Marie ne   As long as she is able to garden,
          s’ennuie jamais.                      Marie is never bored.

The completive or that clause
The that clause completes the main clause by adding an object, subject, attribute, and
so on.

     Que les enfants participent aux tâches      I think it’s important that children
         ménagères me semble important.          participate in daily chores.
       Je suis déçu qu’il pleuve.                     I am disappointed that it’s raining.

   There are various types of these clauses introduced by que or ce que.

       Lucie ne s’attendait pas à ce que Paul cuisine Lucie didn’t expect Paul to cook
             si bien.                                   so well.

The indirect interrogative clause
The hypothetical clause is often used in indirect speech.

       Je me demande si elle aura son bac.    I am wondering if she will graduate fro
                                             high school.
      Nous nous demandons quand leur    We’re wondering when their plane will
         avion arrivera.                          arrive.

The infinitive clauseThe infinitive clause is either used alone or follows a conjugated verb. As expected,
this  clause  always  contains  an  infinitive.  It  is  important  to  note  that  the
corresponding construction in English is the gerundive clause.

      Dîner dans ce restaurant est une     Dining in this restaurant is an
          expérience inouïe.                   extraordinary experience.
      Vous voulez lire vos mails?       Do you want to read your e-mails?

The concessive clause
Implying some kind of concession, from the Latin verb concedo, which among other
things means  I allow or  I concede, this clause adds an element of surprise by
introducing a somewhat unexpected main clause. Affecting the entire main clause,
the concessive clause can either precede or follow  it, sometimes appearing in the
middle of the main clause.
  Words introducing this clause are the following: quoique, bien que, encore que,
que... que, sans que, même si, quand, quand bien même.

      Bien que son ordinateur plante          Although Nathan’s computer crashe
          régulièrement, Nathan refuse d’en       on a regular basis, he refuses to
          acheter un autre.                      buy a new one.
       Qu’il pleuve ou qu’il vente, tu sortiras   Whether it rains or blows, you will
          ton chien!                               take your dog out for a walk!

The consecutive clause
A consecutive clause can be introduced in two ways:

       Either by words illustrating a statement made in the main clause: de façon
      que, de manière que, en sorte que, si bien que, sans que.

     Or by words describing the intensity of an action presented in the main clause:
     à tel/ce point que, à un tel point que, à un point que, si... que, tellement...
      que, tant que, tant/tellement de, tel que, assez/trop/suffisamment... pour
      que.

      Le site web de l’entreprise a été       The company’s web site has been so
         complètement remanié, si bien que    thoroughly overhauled that I canno
            je ne retrouve plus rien.                 find anything anymore.     Ce pays est si endetté que son         This country is so much in debt that its
         économie est fragilisée.             economy has been weakened.

The purpose clause
The purpose clause suggests the idea of the outcome of an action or situation. The
following conjunctions are used: pour que, afin que, à seule fin que, de peur que,
de crainte que.

        J’ai encouragé Margot pour qu’elle   I gave Margot a lot of encouragement so
          obtienne son permis de conduire.     she would get her driver’s license.
       Fabrice ferme la fenêtre de peur que Fabrice closes the window, fearing the
            le vent ne la brise.                 wind might shatter it.

The hypothetical clause
This clause suggests the idea of an uncertain outcome.  It  is introduced by the
following words: si, sauf si, excepté si, même si, comme si, quand, quand même,
quand bien même, à condition que, à supposer que, supposé que, pour peu que,
à moins que, pourvu que, en admettant que, si tant est que, mettons que, soit
que... soit que, selon que, suivant que, au cas où, pour le cas où.

       Mathilde ne viendra pas sauf si vous     Mathilde won’t come unless you cal
           l’appelez.                                   her.
     Au cas où nous devrions attendre au     In case we have to wait in court, let
           tribunal, emportons des journaux.        bring some newspapers.

The relative clause
This clause is always introduced by words denoting relation, such as the following:
que, qui, quoi, dont, où, lequel.

      Manuel aime travailler avec des étudiants   Manuel likes to work with studen
         qui écoutent.                       who listen.
      La maison où ils sont tombés amoureux se  The house where they fell in love
          trouve dans les Pyrénées.                     in the Pyrenees.

The temporal clauseThis clause establishes a temporal link with the main clause, suggesting the ideas of
simultaneity, anteriority, or posterity.
   This clause is introduced by the following words: lorsque, quand, comme, alors
que, pendant que, tandis que, aussi longtemps que, tant que, au moment où, en
même temps que, à présent que, maintenant que, à mesure que, à chaque fois
que, toutes les fois que, avant que, jusqu’à ce que, jusqu’au moment où, en
attendant que, après que, une fois que, dès que, sitôt que, depuis que.

      Tu te seras endormi avant qu’il ne revienne.You will have fallen asleep before
                                               he returns.
      Le coureur No 36 menait le sprint jusqu’au Sprinter No. 36 was leading the
       moment où il a trébuché sur une pierre.    race when he stumbled on a
                                                        stone.

The comparative clause
This clause links two facts or actions by comparing them. It is introduced by the
following  words: comme,  ainsi  que, autant  que, de même  que,  tel  que,
autrement...  que,  plus/moins/aussi/autant...  que,  d’autant  que,  d’autant
plus/moins que, dans la mesure où, selon que, à mesure que.

       Elsa parle plus qu’elle ne chante.          Elsa talks more than she sings.
     Ce tableau est exactement comme je l’avais This painting is exactly as I had
          imaginé.                               imagined it.
