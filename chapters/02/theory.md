## -ir verbs in the present

<!-- PDF pages 31–33 -->

We studied the **-er** verbs in the first chapter. Now, let’s explore the **-ir** and **-re** verbs.
The **-ir** verbs follow two different conjugation patterns:

### Type 1 Verbs (-ir with -iss-)
Type 1 verbs drop the **-ir** of the infinitive, add an **-iss-** to the plural form, and then insert the appropriate ending:

#### Model: *Choisir* (to choose)

| French | English | French | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **je choisis** | I choose | **nous choisissons** | we choose |
| **tu choisis** | you choose | **vous choisissez** | you choose |
| **il/elle choisit** | he/she chooses | **ils/elles choisissent** | they choose |

## The interrogative form

<!-- PDF pages 39–40 -->

In French there are three ways of asking questions:
1. Invert the subject and the verb.
2. Use the **est-ce que** form.
3. Keep the affirmative form with an upward intonation.

### 1. Inversion
- *Comprenez-vous la question ?* (Do you understand the question?)
- *Parlez-vous espagnol ?* (Do you speak Spanish?)

If the third-person singular of a verb ends with a vowel, a **-t-** is inserted to facilitate pronunciation:
- *Parle-t-elle russe ?* (Does she speak Russian?)
- *Voyage-t-il souvent en Europe ?* (Does he often travel to Europe?)
- *Préfère-t-elle voyager en Italie ?* (Does she prefer to travel to Italy?)

### 2. Est-ce que Form
A more colloquial/standard way of asking a question is to place **est-ce que** in front of subject + verb:
- *Est-ce que vous habitez à New York ?* (Do you live in New York?)
- *Est-ce que tu sors ce soir ?* (Are you going out tonight?)

**Est-ce que** becomes **est-ce qu’** before a vowel:
- *Est-ce que vous courez dans le parc ?* (Do you run in the park?)
- *Est-ce qu’ils vendent des timbres ?* (Do they sell stamps?)
- *Est-ce qu’elle apprend le portugais ?* (Does she learn Portuguese?)

### 3. Upward Intonation
In spoken French, you keep the subject + verb order and raise the intonation at the end:
- *Tu finis à cinq heures ?* (You’re finishing at five o’clock?)
- *Vous investissez en Asie ?* (You’re investing in Asia?)

## The negative form

<!-- PDF pages 42–43 -->

To make a sentence negative, place **ne... pas** around the conjugated verb:
- *Elle travaille le lundi.* ➔ *Elle **ne** travaille **pas** le lundi.*
- *Il répond à la lettre.* ➔ *Il **ne** répond **pas** à la lettre.*

If **ne** precedes a verb starting with a vowel or mute h, **ne** becomes **n’**:
- *J’habite à Strasbourg.* ➔ *Je **n’**habite **pas** à Strasbourg.*
- *Elle aime les marguerites.* ➔ *Elle **n’**aime **pas** les marguerites.*
- *Il encourage ses employés.* ➔ *Il **n’**encourage **pas** ses employés.*

Aside from **ne... pas**, other negations are constructed in the same way:
- *Il **n’**attend **personne**.* (He is not waiting for anybody.)
- *Tu **ne** voyages **jamais** en hiver.* (You never travel during the winter.)
- *Il **n’**a **plus de** patience.* (He has no patience left.)
- *Elle **ne** répond **rien**.* (She does not answer anything / answers nothing.)
- *Ce **n’**est **guère** raisonnable.* (It’s hardly reasonable.)

## The negation ni… ni…

<!-- PDF pages 43–44 -->

The negation **ni… ni…** precedes each noun that it negates, and the particle **ne (n’)** comes before the verb.

### Article Rules:
1. **Definite Articles (*le, la, l’, les*) REMAIN**:
   - *Il aime le café et le chocolat.* ➔ *Il **n’**aime **ni le** café **ni le** chocolat.*
   - *Elle aime le bleu et le jaune.* ➔ *Elle **n’**aime **ni le** bleu **ni le** jaune.*
   - *Ils aiment le thé et le café.* ➔ *Ils **n’**aiment **ni le** thé **ni le** café.*

2. **Indefinite or Partitive Articles (*du, de la, de l’, des, un, une*) DISAPPEAR**:
   - *Elle commande de l’eau et du vin.* ➔ *Elle **ne** commande **ni** eau **ni** vin.*
   - *Il vend des oranges et des mangues.* ➔ *Il **ne** vend **ni** oranges **ni** mangues.*
   - *Elle mange de la viande et du fromage.* ➔ *Elle **ne** mange **ni** viande **ni** fromage.*
