            Indirect speech




Indirect speech is used, both in English and in French, to relate conversational
exchanges or information in the third person.

Direct speech versus indirect speech
In direct speech (le discours direct), one says something or asks a question directly.

       Quentin demande:                   Quentin asks:
        « Où allez-vous? »                 “Where are you going?”
        Pierre dit:                             Pierre says:
        « Le président est à Milan. »         “The president is in Milan.”

   In indirect speech (le discours indirect), the words of one or more people are
reported or a question is asked indirectly. There are no quotation marks.

       Quentin demande où vous allez.     Quentin is asking where you are going.
        Pierre dit que le président est à     Pierre is saying that the president is in
         Milan.                            Milan.

If the direct speech is in the present tense, the dependent clause verb is changed into
the imparfait.

       Elle est en Inde.                     She is in India.
        J’ai entendu dire qu’elle était en Inde.   I heard she was in India.

        J’ai entendu dire que tu travaillais pour I heard you were working for France
